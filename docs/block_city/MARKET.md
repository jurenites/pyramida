**Recommendation: continue to a small external playtest, not full production yet.** The promising proposition is a short, understandable vertical planning puzzle in which rooms must fit *and* function. A large combination of colony simulation, decoration, electrical engineering and a deck-builder would be expensive to explain and develop before that proposition is proven.

Do not compare the four live concurrent counts as a controlled experiment. Release timing, discounts, update cadence, session length, content volume and audience size differ. A short premium creative game can have few concurrent players after buyers finish it. Peak concurrency, reviews, sales, profitability and retention are separate measures. No revenue estimate, reliable wishlist total, acquisition cost or conversion forecast is available here.

## Closer positioning checks

**Block'hood is a particularly important comparison.** It already presents a neighbourhood made from interdependent building blocks, with over 200 blocks in its store description. The novelty cannot simply be “a city made of cubes with needs.” Our proposed difference is constrained polycube supply, persistent top/bottom placement, scarce entrances and short progression stages. That differentiation remains a hypothesis. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/416210/Blockhood/)

**ISLANDERS** establishes a minimalist city-building strategy proposition with concise sessions and limited building decisions. It is a better scope reference for the first public slice than a mature colony simulator. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1046030/ISLANDERS/), [publisher site](https://playislanders.com/islanders1)

**Urbek City Builder** emphasizes neighbourhood development, resource constraints and education enabling more efficient structures. It is a useful comparison for whether schools improve the economic loop rather than merely act as a checkbox. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1411740/Urbek_City_Builder/)

**Dystopika** explicitly offers creative city building without goals or management. Its existence means “give construction a purpose” is a choice of audience, not a correction to all builders. The proposal here targets players who want light planning pressure; it should not assume pure decoration players want scarcity. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2379910/Dystopika/)

## Candidate promise and audience

Working pitch: **Fit a living neighbourhood into a tiny city plot. Stack rooms, keep their doors reachable, and bring power up from below.** “Block City” is only a working prototype title; no naming or trademark clearance has been performed.

Primary audience hypothesis: players who enjoy spatial puzzles and compact city-building decisions but do not want time pressure. Secondary hypothesis: vertical-builder players who like shaping dense, improvised neighbourhoods. Decoration-first players may need much more visual authorship than this version provides. Expert factory players may find the current electricity system too shallow. Trying to satisfy both groups immediately would blur the proposition.

A trailer test should show one decision: a tempting room fits, its door would be blocked, the player rotates it, routes access, and the neighbourhood lights up. A basement transformer then enables the next floor. Show cause and effect before showing a large attractive city. Test a second clip focusing on pure shape packing to learn whether connectivity actually adds interest.

## Evidence needed before more production

Run two rounds of five first-time players, roughly 15–25 minutes each. Include spatial-puzzle players and city-builder players. Record consented observations locally; this prototype sends no telemetry. First establish a user-approved goal presentation, because hidden completion criteria confound an unassisted test.

These are proposed decision thresholds, not industry benchmarks:

| Question | First-round signal | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can players act without a controls lecture? | At least 8/10 place and connect a room within two minutes after one brief premise | Improve preview, doors and contextual affordances |
| Does the block create an understandable purpose? | At least 7/10 describe why homes need services and power | Simplify the objective and service feedback |
| Is the first stage forgiving? | At least 7/10 finish it within 15 minutes with no more than one hint | Rebalance cards and costs; examine recoveries |
| Do players plan spatially? | At least 6/10 voluntarily rotate or tip to preserve a later option | Stronger topology choices; do not add content yet |
| Does connectivity justify its clicks? | At least 6/10 prefer the connected version over a packing-only comparison | Simplify or remove the weaker network |
| Is there a reason for another block? | At least 5/10 voluntarily start another run or lot after the session | Rework progression and layout variation |

Track time to first valid placement, first successful connection, first transformer, undo count, blocked-door placements, refits, longest unproductive streak, stage completion and voluntary continuation. Separate confusion from a deliberate hard decision. Ask what players wanted to do but could not. Ten people provide directional usability evidence, not a market-size estimate.

Only after the loop passes should a public demo test wishlists and return play. Keep price and scope decisions provisional until there is evidence of demand and a production cost estimate. The current conclusion is **mechanically testable and worth validating**, with commercial viability still unproven.
